## Help creators choose a clip—not just make more of them.

For this OpusClip concept, I started with a question: once a long video becomes several short clips, how does a creator decide which one is worth posting?

### The problem I wanted to solve

Clipping saves editing time. Choosing what to share is still a separate decision. I wanted to help creators find moments that answer questions their audience is already asking, rather than give them another score to trust.

### The choices I made

I kept OpusClip’s main promise at the centre: turn long videos into short clips. Audience questions became one focused addition, not a new product competing for attention.

I also wanted people to check the recommendation themselves. The comment, source passage, and clip sit together. A partial answer is labeled partial. If there is no supported answer, the experience says so.

### How I addressed it

I brought that idea into a redesigned homepage and a workbench. One agent researches audience questions; a separate agent reviews candidate clips. The creator sees the questions before choosing to generate clips, and acknowledges the credit cost before a paid generation action.

That separation matters. It gives the creator a decision to make—not just a button that starts everything at once.

### What I would do next

This is a prototype, not a measured improvement. I would first make the full handoff reliable, then watch creators use it: do they agree that the clip answers the question, and does it help them decide what to post?
